# HD-1910 servo variant

The stock Microduck body on Feetech **HD-1910-C001** servos (same footprint as
the XL330, so a drop-in), trained with the velocity recipe. Branch `hd1910`.

- Tasks: `Mjlab-Velocity-Flat-MicroDuck-HD1910` and
  `Mjlab-Velocity-Flat-Backlash-MicroDuck-HD1910` (experiment name
  `velocity_hd1910`). Same 61-D obs / 14-target action contract as every other
  policy in the family.
- Built as an **overlay** on `make_microduck_velocity_env_cfg()` through
  `make_hd1910_variant` (`tasks/hd1910.py`). Keep HD-1910-specific changes in
  that overlay, the actuator subclasses and the HD-1910 robot files, not in the
  shared velocity cfg, so merges from `upstream/develop` stay cheap.

What iteration 3 found, in order (plan and execution log:
`~/.claude/plans/define-a-good-scope-rippling-russell.md`):

1. **Fix C** (fine yaw term faded out between 0.10 and 0.20 m/s of linear command)
   recovered top speed: the term also billed the yaw wobble of a fast stride.
2. **Exploration noise hid every start failure.** PPO's learned action noise
   (0.20–0.33 rad per joint at action scale 1.0) kicks a standing robot into its
   first step, so training never charged the deterministic policy for failing to
   start. From a quiet stand C s7@4000 stayed still in 31 % / 82 % of envs
   (fwd 0.10 / turn 0.5) without noise and 2 % / 2 % with it. The action-rate
   penalty was almost entirely noise (2·Σσ² ≈ 1.7 of −1.6 /s). Mdp.py Patch 6 caps
   the std after each update (0.15 → 0.05 by 3250, arm CQN).
3. **The harness only gated forward starts from rest.** CQN s42 could not start
   backward (67 % of robots stay still). `eval_walk.py` now gates `step 0->back
   0.10` and `step 0->left 0.10`; `scripts/start_probe.py` measures all six
   directions on 512 randomized robots.
4. **The reward preferred standing for short small commands.** Walk − stand over
   a 0.10 m/s command plus the 2 s stand after it: −1.1 / −0.3 / +2.7 reward·s for
   2.75 / 4 / 9 s commands; negative at 0.05 m/s even over 9 s. The biggest cost
   was the stock angular term's instant w_x² + w_y² (stride rocking, ~1 /s), then
   pose and the instant linear error. Start drills (arm CQD, short 1.5–4 s
   segments) therefore taught every seed to ignore small commands (98–100 %).
5. **CQR** scores the stock linear term and the rocking part of the stock angular
   term on a 0.5 s EMA (yaw error stays instant; all-EMA, arm CQS, overshot fast
   turns with hip-yaw torque peaks up to 1.12 N·m). Walk − stand becomes positive
   from 2.75 s commands at 0.10 m/s in every direction. Plus: noise cap, 25 %
   quiet episodes (held head/body pose, pushes only while moving), start practice
   after stands, all from iteration 2500.

**Result:** starts are solved on all three seeds (0–3 % stay still in every
direction at every late checkpoint) and every gated row passes on all three. The
final bar (all rows on ≥ 3 of 5000/5500/5750/6000, probe ≤ 10 %, fwd 0.20 / 0.30
≥ 0.17 / 0.25, sensitivity ≤ 4, contract, head tracking ≥ 95 %) passes on s7
(@5500) and fresh s123 (@5000), and **fails on s42 on sensitivity alone** (6–9
fails at every late checkpoint). Across the 10 late CQR checkpoints the
sensitivity fails are kick falls under servo mismatch: gain ×4 10/10, friction ×2
9/10, delay 8–12 6/10, some inside the trained DR range (friction ×1.5, gain ×0.7,
6.5 V with sag). CQN s42@6000 (same cap and quiet episodes, instant stock terms)
failed only gain ×4, so the smoothed rocking penalty likely costs push stability
when the servos are off (hypothesis, not tested). Push robustness under servo
mismatch is the open item; the bench fit should come first, so it targets the
real servo. Results: `runs/pipeline/iter3f.md` (CQR), `iter3e.md` (CQS),
`iter3d.md` (CQD); every checkpoint's row in `docs/results/hd1910_gates.md`.
Report with videos: claude.ai artifact `9gETTXqigcmKt1jN2H5war` (HD-1910 Iteration 3).

*robust* = no fall, no dead zone, speeds within 0.03 m/s and standing torque
within 15 % of nominal, every other gated row passes; *fails* = a gated fall or
a 0.10 m/s command giving < 0.03 m/s (v1 fails every row on its 0.10 dead zone,
so only its falls are shown). Push falls are at the 0.3 m/s kick while walking.

What this says about `hd1910_v2b_5500`, and what the bench must measure:

- **Firmware gain is the dominant axis.** At ×0.7 the robot falls at the 0.3 m/s
  kick in all three replicas (v1 does not), at ×0.5 back/left 0.10 go dead, at
  ×4 it cannot stand. Above nominal it walks faster but the substep peak passes
  0.9 N·m from ×1.3. The P-register → duty/rad mapping is unmeasured and may be
  coarse (P ≈ 3 if it scales like the STS3215), so the bench must measure it to
  ~±15 % and the runtime must pick the P closest to the trained stiffness.
- **Friction second.** ×0.5 is harmless, ×1.5 adds ~25 % standing torque, ×2
  brings back the slow dead zone, halves turning and falls once. Borrowed from
  the XL330: measure it to ~±30 %.
- **Barely matters:** kt and R ±20 %, rotor inertia ×0.5–2 (one push fall in
  three at ×1.5, none at ×2: borderline, not a trend), battery 6.5–8.2 V (slow
  end −0.03 m/s at 0.30), ±1° backlash per joint, a second step of joint
  velocity lag.
- **Latency:** 25–40 ms command-to-motion is fine; 40–60 ms halves turning.
  The real servo's response plus the runtime's bus time must stay under ~40 ms.

`hd1910_v2c` (below) widens the training DR on exactly the first two axes.

## Servo in one paragraph

Stall 0.98–1.18 N·m and continuous 0.29 N·m at 6 V, 9.6 rad/s no-load, 1.6 A
stall, 21 g, 4.0–8.4 V. Feetech **HLS** protocol (not STS), 4096 counts/rev,
current in 6.5 mA units, factory position gains P 32 / D 40, firmware 3.46. The
"max torque" register (16, default 980 × 0.1 %) caps PWM duty, hence
`max_pwm = 0.98`; the protection current (reg 28) unloads the servo after the
over-current time (reg 38), it does not clamp, hence `max_current = None`.
This robot is thermally limited, not torque limited.
